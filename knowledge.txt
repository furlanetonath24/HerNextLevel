## Program 31: MIT PRIMES 
- Organization: Massachusetts Institute of Technology 
- Location: Remote track open nationally/internationally; in-person track requires proximity to Boston 
- Cost: Free 
- Dates: Year-long program (roughly October through May)
- Application Deadline: Typically due in December 
- Target Audience: High school juniors (some tracks open to sophomores) with strong math/CS background. 
- Description: A free, highly selective research program where students work on original research in mathematics, computer science, or computational biology under MIT mentors, culminating in a paper. 


## Program 32: MIT Beaver Works Summer Institute (BWSI) 
- Organization: MIT 
- Location: MIT campus (Cambridge, MA) and online, residential and remote tracks available
- Cost: Free for families earning under $200,000/year; a fee applies above that threshold 
- Dates: Summer, typically late June through early August 
- Application Deadline: Rolling, applications generally open in winter 
- Target Audience: Rising high school seniors with strong STEM background. 
- Description: Intensive STEM/engineering program with tracks in robotics, autonomous systems, cybersecurity, and AI, including hands-on build projects. 


## Program 33: Google Computer Science Summer Institute (CSSI)
- Organization: Google
- Location: Varies by year — check current cycle for virtual vs. in-person 
- Cost: Free - Dates: Summer, typically a few weeks 
- Application Deadline: Typically due in spring

## Program 34: Congressional App Challenge 
- Organization: U.S. House of Representatives (run by local congressional districts) 
- Location: Online (submission-based, tied to student's congressional district) 
- Cost: Free 
- Dates: Opens over the summer, submissions typically due in early November 
- Application Deadline: Rolling within the submission window (varies by district)
- Target Audience: Middle and high school students in the U.S., no experience required. 
- Description: Students create and submit an original app addressing a challenge relevant to their congressional district. Winning teams are recognized nationally, and winning apps are displayed in the U.S. Capitol for one year. 


## Program 35: JHU APL ASPIRE 
- Organization: Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory 
- Location: Laurel, MD (in-person; students must arrange own transportation) 
- Cost: Free (unpaid — no stipend) 
- Dates: June 23–August 21 (roughly) 
- Application Deadline: Typically due in winter 
- Target Audience: High school students interested in engineering/tech research; highly selective (about 7% acceptance rate). 
- Description: Students work full-time hours (30–40/week) alongside APL engineers and scientists on real research projects. One of the most selective free high school research internships in the country. 


## Program 36: Botball / KIPR 
- Organization: KISS Institute for Practical Robotics (KIPR) 
- Location: Regional and international tournaments across the U.S. 
- Cost: Free to low-cost depending on region/team funding 
- Dates: Build season in spring, tournaments through spring/summer 
- Application Deadline: Rolling / team-based sign-up 
- Target Audience: Middle and high school students interested in robotics, no experience required. 
- Description: Teams design, build, and program autonomous robots to compete in tournament challenges — a strong hands-on entry point into robotics and embedded programming. 


## Program 37: FIRST Robotics Competition (FRC) / FIRST Tech Challenge (FTC) - Organization: FIRST (For Inspiration and Recognition of Science and Technology) 
- Location: School- or community-based teams across the U.S. and internationally 
- Cost: Often free to join a team (team itself covers most costs via sponsorships/school funding) - Dates: Build season roughly January–April, competitions through spring 
- Application Deadline: Rolling — join a local team any time 
- Target Audience: High school students (FTC also open to younger students), no experience required. 
- Description: Students join a team to design, build, and program a competitive robot. Widely recognized by colleges and a strong community-based entry point into robotics and engineering.

## Program 57: NCWIT Aspirations in Computing (AiC) High School Award
- Organization: National Center for Women & Information Technology (NCWIT)
- Location: Online application; national and regional/affiliate recognition across all 50 states, DC, Puerto Rico, Guam, and Canada
- Cost: Free
- Dates: Application opens each fall; honorees announced the following spring
- Application Deadline: Typically late October — check current cycle
- Target Audience: High school students in grades 9-12 who are 13 or older, girls, genderqueer, and non-binary students specifically.
- Description: Honors students for their computing-related achievements, leadership, and future plans, not just contest performance. Winners join a long-term community (the AiC Community) with access to scholarships, internships, and near-peer outreach opportunities. Good recommendation for a student building a "computing resume" even without a big flagship project yet.

## Program 6: Regeneron Science Talent Search (STS)
- Organization: Society for Science, sponsored by Regeneron
- Location: Online application; finalists travel to Washington, D.C.
- Cost: Free to enter
- Dates: Application opens over the summer, due in November
- Application Deadline: Mid-November — check current cycle
- Target Audience: High school seniors only, U.S. citizens/permanent residents (or students at eligible US-affiliated schools).
- Description: The oldest and most prestigious pre-college science and math research competition in the US. Requires an independent research project already completed — best suited as a "next step" recommendation for a student already doing research (e.g., after MITES, RSI, or a school science program), not a first entry point.


## Program 75: VEX Robotics Competition (VRC)
- Organization: Robotics Education & Competition (REC) Foundation
- Location: Regional, state, and national events across the US and internationally; World Championship (recently held in cities like Dallas)
- Cost: Registration and team equipment fees apply (some sponsorship/scholarship support available through local teams and REC Foundation grants)
- Dates: Season runs roughly fall through spring, with the World Championship in spring
- Application Deadline: Rolling — join a team any time during the season
- Target Audience: High school students specifically (VRC is the high-school division; VEX IQ is the separate middle-school division).
- Description: Teams of 2-3 students build and program a robot using the VEX V5 platform, competing in alliance-based matches with an autonomous period and driver-controlled period, plus a separate solo Robot Skills Challenge.


## Program 76: World Robot Olympiad (WRO) — Senior High Category
- Organization: World Robot Olympiad Association
- Location: National qualifiers in 95+ countries, feeding into an international final (location rotates yearly)
- Cost: Registration fees apply, varies by country/team
- Dates: National competitions through the year, international final typically in fall
- Application Deadline: Set by each country's national qualifying competition
- Target Audience: The Senior High age category specifically covers ages 14-19 (WRO also has separate Elementary and Junior High categories for younger students, which are not part of this recommendation).
- Description: An international robotics competition (RoboMission, RoboSports, Future Innovators, or Future Engineers tracks) using LEGO-based robotics platforms, with qualification through national competitions.


## Program 77: Genesys Works
- Organization: Genesys Works
- Location: Multiple US cities (e.g., Houston, Chicago, Twin Cities, Bay Area, New York, Seattle — check which cities currently run programs)
- Cost: Free (and pays students a stipend during the internship phase)
- Dates: Summer training followed by a school-year paid internship
- Application Deadline: Check current cycle by city
- Target Audience: High school juniors and seniors specifically, prioritizing students from under-resourced communities.
- Description: Combines a summer technical/professional skills training bootcamp with a paid corporate internship during senior year, aimed at building a direct pathway from high school into a tech-adjacent career or college.


## Program 78: USA Computing Olympiad (USACO)
- Organization: USA Computing Olympiad
- Location: Online, worldwide
- Cost: Free
- Dates: Four online contests per year (December, January, February/March, open division in between), roughly monthly
- Application Deadline: Rolling — register any time before a contest
- Target Audience: Pre-college students (in practice, almost entirely high schoolers), no citizenship restriction to participate, though official USA team selection is US-specific.
- Description: A free, self-paced competitive programming ladder (Bronze through Platinum divisions) that lets a student progress from beginner to advanced algorithmic programming at their own speed — a strong option for a student who wants to go deep on programming specifically, not just explore broadly.


## Program 79: CyberPatriot — Open Division / All Service Division
- Organization: Air & Space Forces Association
- Location: Online, with regional and national final rounds in the US
- Cost: Free to low registration fee per team (varies by year)
- Dates: Season runs roughly October through spring, with the national finals in spring

- Description: The largest cyber defense competition in the US for high schoolers — teams act as IT professionals tasked with finding and fixing cybersecurity vulnerabilities in simulated networks, a strong entry point for a student specifically drawn to cybersecurity over general coding.


## Program 80: American Computer Science League (ACSL) — Senior Division
- Organization: American Computer Science League
- Location: Contests run through participating schools, US and international
- Cost: School/team registration fee applies (typically covered by the school's CS program)
- Dates: Multiple contests through the school year, roughly October–April
- Application Deadline: Set by each participating school's registration
- Target Audience: The Senior Division is specifically for high school students (ACSL also has separate Elementary, Classroom, and Junior divisions not included here).
- Description: A long-running, school-based programming and computer science contest series testing both programming problems and CS theory (data structures, algorithms, digital logic), good for a student already comfortable with a school CS class who wants to compete regularly through the year.
